## Charcoal figure series

These are three pieces from a recent series of figure drawings. I specifically wanted the series to feel like a collection, like they all go together in terms of a shared design direction. I often feel a tension between repetition and variety in my work. I really enjoy variety and exploring different directions, so part of me resists repeating a particular approach. But sometimes I look back at a few weeks of drawings and everything feels a bit scattered and directionless. With this series, I wanted to settle in to a particular design direction I enjoy and repeat it several times to create a series.

In this case, each drawing is the same size and paper, and the design features primarily light backgrounds and emphasizes a strong contour and dynamic poses.

## Gesture Drawing Book

I'd also like to share something a little different this time... an excerpt from a short book I'm writing about gesture drawing! It's just a few sample pages, and I hope a little reading is ok as a break from visual coaching :)

In my own practice, I've found gesture to be one of the most mysterious and confusing drawing concepts to learn. I've also enjoyed teaching gesture drawing workshops over the past year and attempting to make it easier to understand and practice for students. I wanted to distill what I've learned so far into a more tangible resource beyond workshops. I've also felt somewhat dissatisfied with most books about gesture drawing that I've read, and I'd like to see if I can offer another approach that can make gesture more accessible.

These pages are from the first section of the book where I discuss the definition of gesture itself and why it is so elusive and difficult to describe in words. Feedback on the text iself would be appreciated of course, but I don't expect you to be my editor! I'm more interested in hearing more of your own thoughts about gesture related to your own work. My main question for you is: _how do you define gesture?_
